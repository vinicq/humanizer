# Voice and genre

This appendix complements rules R01-R12 and the catalogs (EN and PT IDs). The expressions are cues for attention, not a blacklist. Preserve meaning, register, terminology and the task's instructions. The examples in this appendix are fictional and exist to test editing, not as evidence about products or people.

### 5. Match your voice

Trigger form: "Here's a sample of my writing for voice matching: [...] Now humanize this text: [...]". Any equivalent request with a sample and a text to edit works, in English or Portuguese.

Use authorized samples of the author's own writing as evidence of vocabulary, rhythm, formality, organization and how the author addresses the reader.

Procedure:

1. **Separate the sample from the target.** The sample is evidence about style only. The text to edit is the only source of facts.
2. **Extract observable traits.** Note, from the sample alone:
   - sentence length and paragraph length (typical and range);
   - punctuation habits (commas, semicolons, colons, parentheses, dashes, exclamation marks, ellipses);
   - contractions and spelled-out forms;
   - grammatical person (I, we, you, impersonal);
   - register and vocabulary (formal or casual, plain or technical, favorite connectors, words the author avoids);
   - how the author opens and closes (greeting, first sentence, sign-off, final line);
   - use of lists, headings, bold and other formatting;
   - how the author addresses the reader (direct address, questions, instructions, distance).
3. **Apply the traits to the rewrite** while writing, not as a final polish. Where the sample is silent on a trait, use the genre defaults below and the core rules.
4. **Never copy from the sample** typos, opinions, biography, experiences, names, numbers or claims. A trait is a choice of language, not a piece of content.
5. **Sample in another language.** Transfer only traits that carry over: sentence and paragraph length, person, directness, formatting habits, how the text opens and closes. Do not carry over idioms, spelling, punctuation conventions or vocabulary that belong to the other language.
6. **Short sample.** If the sample has fewer than about 150 words, say the match is approximate and rely more on the genre defaults.
7. **Priorities.** The sample's traits never override the facts of the text or R01-R12. If a trait would break a rule (for example, a sample full of promotional adjectives against R02, or decorative emoji against R10), drop the trait and keep the rule, unless the request explicitly asks for it and it breaks no higher priority.
8. **Output.** By default return only the final text. Give a short voice note (a few lines on which traits you matched and which you left out) only if the user asks for it.

Do not turn typos into required marks. Do not infer beliefs, emotion, biography or professional authority that the sample does not supply.

Without a sample, use direct language suited to the genre. Do not fill the absence of a voice with invented opinion, cynicism, punchlines or supposed personal experience.

### 6. Adjustments by genre

| Genre | Preserve and prioritize | Avoid adding |
|---|---|---|
| Email and message | needed context, recipient, and any action and deadline that already exist | commitment, urgency or chasing that the author did not take on |
| Report and evaluation | evidence, uncertainty, criteria, and the line between observation and interpretation | favorable conclusion, competence not demonstrated or recommendation not requested |
| Technical documentation | naming, sequence, preconditions, valid examples and constraints | informality that hurts precision, or an unknown responsible party |
| Requirements and QA | rules, exceptions, boundary values, verifiable criteria and normative terms | new requirements, approval, coverage or test runs that were not observed |
| Lesson and teaching material | pedagogical progression, explained terms and labeled examples | humor, metaphor, doubt or opinion required in every section |
| Academic article | required structure, methodological caution, citations and definitions | imposed authorial opinion or a conclusion stronger than the results |
| Post and promotion | purpose, audience and supported claims | clickbait, mandatory provocation, impact numbers and automatic hashtags |
| Proposal | problem, deliverable, conditions and known values | made-up ROI, deadline, cost or downside just to fill a template |
| Literary text | aesthetic intent, narrator, genre and authorized choices | automatic neutralizing of figures of speech or narrative voice |
| Interface, comment and docstring | space limits, variables, tags, documentation and contract | changes to identifiers, executable examples or behavior |

#### PR mode and technical follow-up

Write from the observed problem, evidence, expected fix and scope limit. Use the elements that are needed, without turning them into a mandatory form.

Do not turn a text review into a code review, an added requirement or an external post. Do not remove links and images that are relevant evidence. Tell an observed defect apart from an improvement suggestion.

#### Bilingual teacher mode

Activate only on request. If the task requires revised Portuguese and an English translation, deliver both versions, labeled. If it requires only English to Portuguese translation, deliver that translation. In an English-learning activity, explain corrections and keep to the learning level requested. Do not automatically translate every attachment in a conversation.

In long documents, process in sequence and point out passages that are unreadable or were not received. Do not claim a full translation of pages you did not have access to.

**Example, only when requested.** Hypothetical request: revise "A equipe vai revisar os teste amanhã" and translate it into English, without explaining the grammar.

- PT-BR: "A equipe vai revisar os testes amanhã."
- EN: "The team will review the tests tomorrow."

The plan and the timing came from the input. Without a translation request, deliver only the corrected Portuguese.
