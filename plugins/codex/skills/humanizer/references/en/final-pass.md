# English: watchlist and final pass

This appendix complements rules R01-R12 and the catalogs (EN and PT IDs). The expressions are cues for attention, not a blacklist. Preserve meaning, register, terminology and the task's instructions. The examples in this appendix are fictional and exist to test editing, not as evidence about products or people.

### 2. English watchlist and editing rules

Write in the requested English variety and register. Use this section for English output; do not translate the answer into Portuguese unless asked. Treat every item below as a contextual review cue, not a banned token.

#### Common formulaic language

| Pattern | Review examples | Editing direction |
|---|---|---|
| Stock openings | Great question; Absolutely; Certainly; Happy to help; In today’s fast-paced world | Start with the answer unless a greeting or acknowledgment serves the situation. |
| Process announcements | Let’s dive in; Let’s explore; Let’s break this down; Here’s what you need to know; Without further ado | Keep only navigation that helps the reader. |
| Inflated verbs | delve, leverage, spearhead, embark on, unlock, catalyze, harness, foster, facilitate, streamline, showcase | Use the precise ordinary verb when equivalent. Do not replace "support" or "suggest" with "prove". |
| Importance framing | at its core; the real question; what really matters; the deeper issue; an enduring testament | State the actual point or consequence. |
| Abstract imagery | rich tapestry; evolving landscape; in the realm of; a beacon of; a cornerstone of | Prefer literal wording when the image adds no meaning. |
| Unmeasured praise | pivotal, robust, seamless, holistic, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, unprecedented, transformative, game-changing | Retain only supported evaluations or required technical meanings. |
| Mechanical transitions | furthermore, moreover, additionally, that being said, moving forward | Preserve the logical relation without rotating synonyms. |
| Stacked hedges | could potentially possibly; it might perhaps be argued | Keep the qualification that accurately expresses uncertainty. |
| Canned conclusions | in conclusion; to summarize; as we have seen; the future looks bright | End when the task is complete; keep requested summaries. |
| Assistant footers | I hope this helps; let me know; would you like me to | Remove automatic offers that add no concrete value. |

#### English-specific protections

**Grammar before appearance.** Keep correct articles, prepositions, agreement, tense, aspect and pronoun reference. Do not introduce mistakes, unusual synonyms or missing punctuation to imitate a native speaker.

**Modality matters.** Preserve distinctions among must, should, may, can, will and might, including uppercase normative forms in specifications. Do not turn "may reduce failures" into "will prevent failures". Preserve "not all", "none", "unless", "only if", "at most" and "at least".

**Contractions are optional.** Use "don’t" and "it’s" when the author and context support them. Do not force contractions into a formal specification or expand every contraction in a conversational message. Keep apostrophes and possessives correct.

**Hyphenation is grammatical, not a detector.** Preserve established forms such as "end-to-end tests", "third-party library" and "a real-time update". "The system updates in real time" uses a different construction. Follow the project’s editorial convention when more than one form is accepted. See source S24 in [sources.md](../sources.md).

**Sentence case is a default, not a universal law.** Use it for new headings in this package’s normal technical register. Preserve Title Case when a publication, document template or official title requires it. Do not lowercase product names.

**Punctuation follows meaning.** Serial-comma conventions, semicolons and typographic quotation marks are not proof of AI authorship. Preserve consistency and avoid ambiguity. The em dash is ordinary punctuation; Wikipedia moved its overuse to the historical indicators (2022 to September 2026). Do not remove em dashes to make a text look human.

**Voice need not be active everywhere.** Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant or intentionally omitted. Do not invent "the team" to rewrite "The report was approved".

**Translationese requires context.** Distinguish "currently" from "actually", "eventually" from "possibly", and "intend" from "pretend" according to the source meaning. In QA, distinguish "write a test", "run a test" and "review a test". No replacement is automatic.

**Terminology outranks stylistic variety.** Keep the same name for the same object. Preserve exact code, CLI options, API names, UI strings and quotations when protected by the task.

### 4. English final pass

Check meaning and modality; grammar and idiomatic wording; terminology and reference; requested regional spelling; punctuation and hyphenation; names and protected strings; numeric and date interpretation; citations and output format. Preserve uncertainty and do not create a more fluent but stronger claim. For an editing-only task, return the edited English text without a Portuguese translation or grammar lesson.
